1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================

The Guardian Drone System is an end-to-end autonomous safety platform that:
  - Detects physiological distress signals from a smartwatch
  - Autonomously dispatches a drone from the nearest police station
  - Tracks the victim even if she is moving in a vehicle
  - Analyzes the scene using AI computer vision
  - Notifies authorities
  — without any human operator involvement from trigger to arrival.

THE CORE INNOVATION
No single piece of this technology is new in isolation. What does not exist is
the fully integrated AI-orchestrated pipeline:

  wearable biometric detection
    → autonomous police-station drone dispatch
    → moving victim tracking
    → scene intelligence (building entry/exit analysis)
    → fault-tolerant multi-drone handoff
    → government management dashboard

This orchestration layer — built with LangGraph, MAVLink, and YOLOv8 — is the
patentable, novel contribution of this project.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 PROBLEM STATEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In India, a woman is assaulted every 16 minutes (NCRB data).
The critical gap is between incident and police response:
  - Urban areas  : 10–30 minutes average response time
  - Rural areas  : 1–3 hours average response time

Existing solutions (panic buttons, helpline apps) require conscious manual action
from a woman who may be incapacitated or in too much danger to reach for her phone.

This system eliminates that requirement entirely by detecting distress automatically
from involuntary physiological signals.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.2 HOW IT WORKS — SEVEN STEPS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Smartwatch continuously monitors HRV, GSR, breathing rate, and motion every 5s.
2. AI model computes a danger score (0–100) against the user's personal baseline.
3. If score > 70 for 15+ sustained seconds with 2+ signals in distress range,
   SOS triggers automatically — no manual action needed.
4. AI agents identify the nearest police drone station, compute optimal flight path,
   and dispatch the drone autonomously.
5. If victim is moving in a vehicle, drone tracks GPS in real time.
6. If drone malfunctions, AI reroutes to next station and dispatches a second drone,
   handing off all context within 30 seconds.
7. On arrival, AI vision agent classifies the scene, maps building entries and exits,
   tracks the threat, and streams live video to police command.
   Police arrive with full situational awareness.
   Drone logs all evidence. Mission closes. Drone returns autonomously.

PHASE 1 — BIOMETRIC ML DANGER DETECTION MODEL
Duration: Month 1–2
Goal: Build and validate the AI model that converts raw smartwatch signals
      into a reliable danger score.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 1.1 — DATASET ACQUISITION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Primary:
  - Download WESAD dataset from UCI ML Repository
  - URL: https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/465/wesad
  - 15 subjects, chest + wrist sensors, labeled stress / baseline / amusement
  - Contains: BVP (for HRV), EDA (GSR), accelerometer, temperature

Secondary:
  - Download DREAMER and DEAP from PhysioNet as supplementary data

Setup:
  - Create folder structure: data/raw/, data/processed/, data/features/
  - Write loader script — verify all 15 subject .pkl files load without error
  - Plot sample HRV, EDA, and accelerometer traces for 3 subjects (sanity check)

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  You cannot train a model without data. WESAD gives you 15 real human subjects
  experiencing real physiological stress in a controlled lab — the gold standard
  for this exact problem. Without this data your model is guessing. With it, you
  have a scientifically validated foundation that government reviewers will accept.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 1.2 — SIGNAL PREPROCESSING PIPELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HRV signals:
  - BVP/PPG: band-pass filter 0.5–4 Hz to remove motion artifacts
  - Extract R-R intervals (inter-beat intervals) from PPG peaks
  - Compute: RMSSD, SDNN, pNN50, LF/HF ratio, mean, std, range

GSR signals:
  - EDA: low-pass filter at 1 Hz, separate tonic + phasic components
  - Tonic = slow arousal baseline, phasic = fast threat response spikes
  - Extract: mean, std, peak count, peak amplitude, slope, rise time

Accelerometer:
  - Magnitude = sqrt(x²+y²+z²), band-pass 0.1–0.5 Hz for breathing
  - Estimate breathing rate from oscillation frequency
  - Extract: mean magnitude, std, spectral entropy

Windowing:
  - Apply 30-second sliding window with 5-second step across all signals
  - Remove windows with >20% missing or artifact-contaminated data
  - Write unit tests for all functions — validate against known signal examples

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  Raw sensor signals are dirty — motion artifacts, electrical noise, gaps.
  Feeding dirty signals into a model means the model learns noise, not stress.
  RMSSD (heart rate variability metric) is one of the most researched stress
  biomarkers in medical literature — you need clean signals to compute it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 1.3 — PERSONAL BASELINE CALIBRATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Collect 2 weeks of passive wear data covering normal daily activities
  - Compute per-user statistical profile: mean and std per feature
  - Normalize all features as z-scores relative to personal baseline
  - Implement PersonalBaseline class: fit(), normalize(), save(), load()
  - Use exponential moving average (alpha=0.05) for incremental baseline updates
  - Store baseline on device only — raw biometrics never leave the watch

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  Two people can have identical HRV values — one panicking, one completely calm
  because that is their normal. Population-average model cannot tell them apart.
  Personal baseline measures deviation from that person's own normal — far more
  sensitive. This is the primary false-positive reduction step in the system.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 1.4 — XGBOOST BASELINE CLASSIFIER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Create feature matrix: (N_windows, 16 features) for all subjects
  - Assign binary labels: WESAD label 2 (stress) → distress=1, all others → safe=0
  - Train XGBoost with scale_pos_weight=3 to handle class imbalance
  - Leave-one-subject-out cross-validation (LOSO-CV)
  - Compute SHAP values — plot top 10 most predictive features
  - Target: F1 > 0.78 on distress class
  - Save as baseline_xgb.pkl

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  Before building the LSTM, verify that feature engineering is correct.
  XGBoost trains in minutes and SHAP values show which signals matter most.
  If XGBoost cannot beat 78% F1, something is wrong upstream — fix it here,
  not after 30 epochs of LSTM training.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 1.5 — LSTM DANGER CLASSIFIER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Architecture:
  - 2-layer LSTM, hidden size 128, dropout 0.3
  - Input shape: (batch, 300 timesteps, 5 signals) — BVP, EDA, ACC_x, ACC_y, ACC_z
  - Output: softmax → [P(safe), P(distress)]
  - Danger score = P(distress) × 100

Training:
  - AdamW lr=1e-3, ReduceLROnPlateau scheduler, 30 epochs
  - CrossEntropyLoss with class weights [1.0, 2.5] to penalize missed distress
  - Full LOSO-CV across all 15 subjects
  - Target: mean F1 > 0.83 on distress class
  - Save best checkpoint: best_danger_model.pt

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  Unlike XGBoost which sees extracted statistics, LSTM processes the raw sequence
  and learns temporal patterns: 'HRV dropped sharply while GSR spiked in last 8s'.
  Stress has a time signature that statistics miss. This is the production model —
  runs on the watch companion app every 5 seconds for the rest of the product's life.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 1.6 — CONTEXTUAL GATING LAYER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Multi-signal agreement: require 2 of 3 signals (HRV, GSR, breathing) in distress range
  - Duration gate: sustained score >= 70 for minimum 15 seconds before SOS fires
  - Location safety gate: GPS geofence for known safe zones raises threshold to 85
  - Dismiss window: 10-second window after first trigger — double-tap to cancel
  - Smoothing: rolling average of last 10 danger score readings (50-second window)
  - Test: exercise scenario must NOT trigger, genuine fear scenario MUST trigger

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  A model with 83% accuracy still produces false alarms. In a safety system, a
  false alarm dispatches a police drone unnecessarily — wasting resources and
  eroding trust. The gating layer adds common-sense rules on top of ML output.
  This is the difference between a demo toy and a deployable product.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 1.7 — MODEL EXPORT AND OPTIMIZATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Export trained LSTM to ONNX format: torch.onnx.export()
  - Validate ONNX outputs match PyTorch outputs (< 0.001 tolerance)
  - Apply PyTorch INT8 dynamic quantization — 4x size reduction
  - Benchmark: 1000 inference passes, mean + std. Target: < 50ms per window
  - Write model card: training data, performance, known failure modes, limitations

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  A model that only runs on your laptop is not a product. ONNX export makes it
  runnable on any device — watch companion phone, Jetson Orin Nano, future
  smartwatch chip. INT8 quantization shrinks model 4x, critical for battery life.
  Model card is what government technical reviewers read first.

PHASE 2 — AI ORCHESTRATION AGENTS (LANGGRAPH)
Duration: Month 3–4
Goal: Build the multi-agent system that connects danger score to autonomous

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 2.1 — AGENT ARCHITECTURE DESIGN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Define SharedState TypedDict:
    victim_location, danger_score, drone_status, nearest_station,
    flight_path, mission_status, malfunction_flag, handoff_complete
  - Create LangGraph StateGraph with all 10 agent nodes
  - Define conditional edges: danger threshold check, malfunction check, mission close
  - Draw complete agent graph — save to docs/agent_graph.png
  - Define agent interfaces in Pydantic: every input and output is typed — no raw strings

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  Designing the full agent graph before writing code forces you to confront every
  edge case: no stations available, victim GPS lost, LLM call timeout, two
  malfunctions in sequence. The StateGraph and Pydantic typed outputs mean every
  agent communicates in structured data — not fragile strings that break when an
  LLM rephrases its response.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 2.2 — DRONE STATION REGISTRY (POSTGRESQL + POSTGIS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Set up PostgreSQL with PostGIS extension (via Docker for local development)
  - Schema: station_id, name, lat, lon, drone_count, available_drones,
    battery_levels, last_maintenance
  - Implement geospatial query: nearest N stations within radius R,
    filtered by drone_available=true
  - Build FastAPI REST endpoints:
    GET  /stations/nearest
    POST /stations/{id}/dispatch
    GET  /stations/{id}/status
  - Seed database with 10 mock police station records at real GPS coordinates

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  The system needs to know, in real time, which police drone station is closest
  to the victim, has a charged drone available, and has enough battery for a round
  trip. PostGIS handles geospatial distance queries in milliseconds — critical when
  you need an answer in under 2 seconds after SOS triggers. Without this registry,
  the dispatch agent is blind.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 2.3 — PATH PLANNING AGENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Implement A* pathfinding on a GPS grid overlaid on map tiles
  - Cost function: distance (primary), battery consumption, signal strength, wind
  - Load no-fly zones from OpenAirspace API — block those grid cells
  - Dynamic re-planning: if victim moves > 100m from last known position, recompute
  - Battery range check: if round-trip > 80% battery capacity, flag for handoff
  - Expose as @tool decorated function within LangGraph agent

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  The fastest straight-line path may cross a no-fly zone, go through a building,
  or exceed the drone's battery range. The path planning agent solves this in real
  time. Battery-aware routing is especially critical for the handoff scenario.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 2.4 — MALFUNCTION DETECTION AND HANDOFF AGENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Monitor drone telemetry every 500ms:
    battery voltage, GPS lock, motor RPM, signal RSSI, altitude hold
  - Malfunction triggers:
    battery < 15%, GPS loss > 3 seconds, motor failure code, RSSI < -90 dBm
  - Handoff sequence:
    1. On fault: notify management, find ahead-station (not behind), dispatch Drone 2
    2. Transfer context packet: victim GPS, danger level, mission timestamp, video state
    3. Direct Drone 1 to nearest dockable station
    4. Drone 1 docks, logs fault code, maintenance team alerted automatically
  - All handoff steps must complete within 30 seconds of malfunction detection

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  This is the most innovative engineering contribution. When Drone 1 malfunctions,
  the system must seamlessly transition to Drone 2 without victim coverage gap.
  The handoff agent must find a station AHEAD of the victim's direction of travel —
  not behind — which requires predicting where victim will be when Drone 2 arrives.
  This is what makes this system fundamentally different from any existing product.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 2.5 — MOCK DRONE API
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Build FastAPI mock server:
    POST /launch
    GET  /telemetry
    POST /set_waypoint
    POST /land
    POST /rtl
  - Mock telemetry: GPS coords, battery%, altitude, speed, camera_active,
    motor_status, signal_rssi
  - Fault injection: POST /inject_fault?type=
    Types: battery_low, gps_loss, motor_failure, signal_loss
  - Run 2 mock drone instances on ports 8001 and 8002 for multi-station testing

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  You cannot test agent logic against a real drone — it is dangerous and expensive.
  The mock API is a safe, infinitely controllable drone. The fault injection endpoint
  is the most important feature — trigger battery_low at exactly the right moment
  to test whether your handoff agent reacts correctly.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 2.6 — OPENROUTER LLM CONFIGURATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - All LLM calls: base_url='https://openrouter.ai/api/v1', OPENROUTER_API_KEY from .env
  - All agent decision outputs typed as Pydantic models — never parse raw LLM text
  - Implement retry logic with tenacity: 3 retries, exponential backoff, fallback
  - Test: run a complete agent LLM call, verify Pydantic output, verify retry on timeout

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  Using OpenRouter means you can switch LLM providers (OpenAI → Claude → Mistral)
  in one line of code — if one provider has downtime during a real emergency.
  Pydantic-typed outputs mean agent decisions are machine-readable, not prose.

PHASE 4 — COMPUTER VISION SCENE INTELLIGENCE
Duration: Month 7–8
Goal: Build the AI vision system that analyzes the scene when the drone arrives.
Your strongest domain — YOLOv8 from NIAR experience applied directly.
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 4.1 — SCENE CLASSIFICATION MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Binary classifier: open area (0) vs enclosed/building (1)
  - Training data: Google Open Images V7, 5000 outdoor + 5000 building images
  - Model: MobileNetV3-Small (lightweight, purpose-built for edge devices)
  - Train: 80/20 split, 20 epochs, Adam lr=1e-4, augmentation (flip, brightness, rotation)
  - Target: > 92% accuracy, < 15ms inference per frame on Jetson
  - Save as scene_classifier.pt

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  The scene classifier makes the first decision on arrival: open ground or building?
  Open → hover and track. Building → orbit, scan entries, relay exit map to police.
  Get this wrong and the drone uses the wrong strategy for the environment.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 4.2 — BUILDING ENTRY/EXIT DETECTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Collect and annotate training images: doors, gates, windows, stairwells
  - Annotate in Roboflow (free tier) — export in YOLOv8 bounding box format
  - Train YOLOv8-nano: 50 epochs, img size 640
  - Target: mAP@0.5 > 0.72
  - Orbital scan behavior when building detected:
    1. Increase altitude to 15m for overview
    2. Perform 360-degree slow orbit at fixed radius
    3. Detect and GPS-tag all visible doors, gates, ground-floor windows
    4. Transmit entry/exit map to management dashboard in real time

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  An officer who enters a building without knowing all exits is at risk of ambush.
  Your drone maps every visible door, gate, and window and transmits GPS coordinates
  to police before they arrive. This changes blind tactical entry into coordinated
  approach. Most visually impressive feature for government demonstration.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 4.3 — PERSON DETECTION AND TRACKING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Deploy YOLOv8-medium for person detection (pretrained COCO weights — no retraining)
  - Install ByteTrack: maintains persistent person IDs through occlusions
  - ByteTracker config: max_age=30, min_hits=3, iou_threshold=0.3
  - Victim identification: person whose GPS best matches watch GPS at drone arrival
  - Behavior analysis: flag rapid approach to victim, restraining motion as suspect
  - Stream all bounding boxes, IDs, and role labels to dashboard via WebSocket

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  When drone arrives, there may be multiple people: victim, threat actor, bystanders.
  System must identify which one is the victim and track all persistently. ByteTrack
  maintains IDs even when someone ducks behind a car and reappears — same tracking ID.
  This is what makes the video feed actionable intelligence, not just confusing footage.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 4.4 — NIGHT VISION AND LOW-LIGHT MODE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Detect ambient light from RealSense color stream mean brightness
  - Automatic switch: if mean brightness < 40 lux → activate IR depth mode
  - Apply CLAHE enhancement to low-light RGB frames before running YOLO detection
  - Test in simulated dark conditions: person detection accuracy must remain > 80%

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  Most assaults happen at night. A drone that only works in daylight is not a safety
  system — it is a daylight demonstration. The RealSense D435i has active IR sensing
  that works in complete darkness. Without this module: 12-hour gap in coverage daily.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 4.5 — JETSON ORIN NANO OPTIMIZATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Install JetPack SDK (Ubuntu 22.04 base, includes CUDA and TensorRT)
  - Export scene classifier to TensorRT FP16 engine
  - Export YOLOv8-nano (building detection) to TensorRT INT8 engine
  - Export YOLOv8-medium (person tracking) to TensorRT FP16 engine
  - Benchmark full pipeline on Jetson: target 30 FPS simultaneous inference
  - Implement GPU temperature monitor: throttle to 20 FPS if GPU temp > 80°C
  - Commit all TensorRT engine files to /vision/engines/

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  Running 3 neural networks simultaneously needs efficient CUDA usage. Without
  TensorRT, 30 FPS pipeline becomes 7 FPS slideshow — person tracking becomes
  unusable. TensorRT gives 3–4x speedup on Jetson hardware specifically.

WHY THIS MATTERS:

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 5.2 — PIXHAWK 6C CALIBRATION (5 STEPS — IN ORDER)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Flash latest stable PX4 firmware using QGroundControl
  - Calibration 1: Accelerometer — 6 orientations on flat surface
  - Calibration 2: Compass — figure-8 rotation outdoors away from metal
  - Calibration 3: ESC — all 4 ESCs synchronized simultaneously
  - Calibration 4: Radio — RC transmitter range calibration for safety override
  - Calibration 5: Level horizon — sets true level reference for flight controller

  Safety parameters:
  - Max altitude: 120m (legal limit)
  - RTL battery threshold: 20%
  - Geofence: enabled (200m radius)
  - MAVLink baud 921600 on UART/TELEM2 → connected to Jetson

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  An uncalibrated Pixhawk cannot distinguish true horizontal from a tilted surface,
  cannot read GPS accurately, and cannot synchronize all four motors — leading to
  unstable hover, drift, and crash. Calibration must be done IN ORDER.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 5.3 — JETSON INTEGRATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Mount Jetson on payload rail with vibration-dampening foam between mount and frame
  - Connect Jetson UART to Pixhawk TELEM2 via USB-serial adapter

  - Install all Python dependencies from requirements.txt on Jetson
  - Copy TensorRT vision engine files to /models/ on Jetson
  - Create guardian-agent.service systemd unit — auto-starts on boot, auto-restarts on crash
  - Insert LTE SIM, test cellular data, ping cloud backend — verify latency < 100ms

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  Vibration-dampening foam is not optional — flight vibrations cause persistent
  noise in IMU readings. The systemd service ensures agent software starts

MODULE 5.4 — BENCH TESTING (NO PROPELLERS)

MODULE 6.2 — WATCH COMPANION APP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Wear OS app (Android Studio): background service for continuous biometric collection
  - Run ONNX danger model locally — inference every 5 seconds without cloud dependency
  - Haptic feedback: double-buzz on score 50–70 (uncertain range)
  - 10-second dismiss window: double-tap to cancel if user knows she is safe
  - Auto-SOS: encrypted danger payload sent to cloud if score > 70 for 15 seconds

WHY THIS MATTERS:

  signals that the system respects user judgment — critical for adoption.

MODULE 6.5 — PATENT FILING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - File provisional patent BEFORE any public demonstration or government presentation
  - Key claims:
    1. Biometric autonomous drone dispatch pipeline
    2. Fault-tolerant multi-drone handoff protocol
    3. AI scene intelligence for law enforcement coordination
  - Budget: $1,500–3,000 with patent attorney — non-negotiable
  - After filing: 12 months patent-pending protection
  - File non-provisional within this 12-month window

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  Filing BEFORE any public demonstration is the difference between owning your
  invention and losing it. Public disclosure starts a 12-month clock after which
  you permanently lose patent rights in most countries. Without protection, a larger
  company can observe your government demo and build the same thing.

7.1 WHO TO APPROACH FIRST
  - Always approach State government before Central — they move faster and have
    direct ownership of police operations.

  - India: approach MeitY Startup Hub for technology sandbox approval
  - Apply to DGCA Innovation Sandbox — created for accelerating drone startup approvals
  - Apply to Startup India recognition — reduces regulatory friction

7.2 DGCA REGULATORY REQUIREMENTS (INDIA)
  - Register drone under Digital Sky Platform (mandatory)
  - Apply for NSOP (Non-Scheduled Operator Permit) under Category 3 for medium drones
  - BVLOS (Beyond Visual Line of Sight) waiver required for autonomous dispatch
  - UAS type certificate required for the specific drone configuration
  - Data localization: all data must reside on Indian servers (DPDPA 2023)
  - Recommended first step: DGCA Innovation Sandbox — waives standard approval timeline

7.3 WHAT THE PROTOTYPE MUST DEMONSTRATE TO GOVERNMENT
  - Live working demo (not slides): SOS trigger → drone dispatch → arrival → scene analysis
  - Response time data: show 2–3 minute mean response vs 15–30 minute police car
  - False positive rate: demonstrate < 5% in controlled testing
  - Data privacy: prove no raw biometric data leaves the user's device
  - Safety record: zero hardware incidents across all prototype tests
  - Cost: $1,230 per station vs $50,000+ for conventional alternatives
